The Gas-Station Run Keeps Its Own Clock

A model saw only what sold, never the clock. It still split the day into three runs: morning coffee, a long daytime stretch and late night. And nearly a third of baskets are fuel and nothing else.

For the business: Stock the morning for coffee, energy drinks and pastries, and the day for lottery, fuel and beer. The pump is the biggest untapped aisle: only 1 in 7 fuel baskets holds anything else.

Everyone knows coffee sells in the morning. The real question is how many selling periods a store has, and where one ends and the next begins. We asked a model without telling it the time.

The data: about 980 million receipts from independent convenience stores, via PDI (August 2025 to August 2026, 168 of 673 files). The groups are groups of hours and baskets, not people.

The day sorts itself

We cut the year into 2,184 hour slots and gave a model only each slot's mix across 31 product categories. It found three groups. Read back against the clock, they fall in order: morning (about 3 to 9 a.m.), day and evening (10 a.m. to 8 p.m.), and late night. For 153 of the 168 weekday-hours, all 13 months agreed.

The morning starts later as the week goes on: 3 a.m. Monday to Thursday, 4 a.m. Friday and Saturday, 5 a.m. Sunday. Refits on random 80% slices gave the same groups (agreement 0.99 out of 1).

What each run buys

The morning is the sharpest. Coffee is 3.6% of items sold (1.5% overall), energy drinks 9.0% (5.3%), and beer's share halves. The day leans on lottery, fuel and beer. Late night leans on tobacco, salty snacks and candy.

Raw hourly counts agree without any model: 16.6% of the day's coffee sells in the 7 a.m. hour. Beer peaks at 5 p.m.

Two caveats. The model weighs quiet hours like busy ones: late night is 28% of the slots but only 9% of the items. And three groups only narrowly beat two.

Nearly a third of baskets are fuel and nothing else

30.8% of baskets hold fuel and nothing else. They average $37.48, against $12.41 for a basket with no fuel, so they bring in 54% of basket revenue. Every month falls between 29.6% and 32.1%.

Only about 1 in 7 fuel baskets holds anything else. Some of that is bookkeeping: paying at the pump and then buying a coffee inside can show up as two baskets. Fuel-only peaks at 10 a.m. (34.4% of baskets) and bottoms out at 1 a.m. (22.6%).

Baskets without fuel are small: 2.1 items on average, and 44% are a single item. Cigarettes lead (13.4%), then soft drinks, energy drinks and lottery (about 11% each).

What sells together

Lift compares how often two categories share a basket with how often chance would put them there. 1 means chance. Coffee and sweet baked goods top the list at 3.8×, and it holds every month. But they're in only 0.13% of baskets. Salty snacks and soft drinks pair more loosely (2.7×) and reach six times as many baskets. Cigarettes and fuel meet at a quarter of the rate chance predicts.

That suggests two tests: a coffee-and-pastry deal for a small, tight group, and a snacks-and-soda deal for a looser habit that reaches far more baskets.

Some pairs are just the clock

Coffee and hot food look like a pair, but both simply sell in the morning. Timing alone predicts 1.45× of their 1.51×. Coffee and a pastry keep most of their pull. The oddest real pair is health-and-beauty items with water or sports drinks, strongest in December. Nicotine pouches go with energy drinks but avoid cigarettes.

How this analysis was done

Data. PDI convenience-store line items, August 2025 to August 2026, from 168 of 673 files. Baskets are rebuilt within each file. The stores are participating independents, not a random sample. There are no exact dates, so holidays can't be separated. Time zones are unverified, though the 7 a.m. coffee peak suggests local time. About a quarter of items are unclassified.

Time slots. 2,184 month × weekday × hour slots, each described by its share of item lines across 31 categories, then scaled. K-means was tried for 2 to 9 groups, and 3 scored best (silhouette 0.343, against 0.338 for 2). Five 80% subsamples agreed at 0.99 (adjusted Rand index), and the first and second halves of the year at 0.92. Slots count equally regardless of volume. The late-night group is partly defined by unclassified items.

Baskets and pairs. Plain shares of all 980 million basket records, not clusters. Lift is pooled over 13 months; monthly ranges are the lowest and highest monthly lift. Store-level and category clusters failed the project's own quality checks and are left out. These are patterns, not causes.

Pairs and the clock. The second pair chart counts only baskets with something besides fuel (678 million), because fuel-only baskets inflate every lift by the same factor. Pairs need at least 98,000 shared baskets and 500 in every month, a floor fixed before ranking. Intervals are the wider of two bootstraps (over months and over files). Timing-only lift treats two categories as independent within each month × hour slot. A category network was also tested; the version planned in advance did not beat shuffled networks, so it is left out.
